02What it looks like

Sarah is the COO of a 40-person company. Imagine yours. She spends 4–6 hours every month building the leadership team's monthly business review — pulling numbers from the CRM, finance, and support tool; making the charts; writing the narrative; formatting it for the meeting.

An imagined first run

Cliff pulled the source data. Rebecca built the charts and breakdown tables. Woody wrote the exec narrative. Carla grilled the draft. Sarah reviewed and approved in about 90 minutes — down from 4–6 hours.

Three months later: the report ships the same Monday every month, reviewed in under 90 minutes. The system has learned Sarah's voice, her CEO's priorities, and which sections leadership actually reads — every cycle sharper than the last.

03Why it's different

  • Real files, not chat output. Send the Word doc or deck straight to your client or CFO.
  • Remembers every account. Switch between clients and every agent knows the context.
  • Runs locally. Your data stays on your machine. No cloud dashboard, no privacy risk with client work.
  • Gets sharper with use. Learns your voice, your pricing, your standard terms — the 20th proposal is sharper than the 1st.

04How we got here

Six days, six load-bearing things shipped. The work the page won't show, but should explain.

April 21–22
Five deliverable types shipped. Proposals, monthly business reviews, executive decks, operational audits, research reports. Each one clears its own rubric against the dogfood test scenario.
April 22
Site live. tavernos.ai on Cloudflare, contact email working, the friend-installer page wired to a clean ZIP. The product was real before the page was.
April 23
A test framework that doesn't call the API. The kind of bug that used to take twelve agent runs to find now surfaces in two seconds. Runs on every change. Three tiers: smoke, regression, format coverage.
April 24
Invocation reliability fixed. Got the model to actually use its tools when it should. The same prompts that called the tool one time in four now call it more than four times in five.
April 25
Three ways to bring a file in. Slash command, paperclip button, drag-and-drop. One backend, three entry points, the same chat output afterward.
April 26
Memory scoped per-client. Each client gets its own memory and file index. Nothing leaks across clients. Pre-existing data migrates to a default client on first restart — no re-ingestion.
